# Visual asset provenance and replacement policy

## Decision

The checked-in legacy files are visual evidence, not a redistributable asset library. The rebuild may copy an asset only when an asset-level record identifies its authoritative source, license/permission, permitted product use, required notices, and immutable source hash. Otherwise it must use the parity-safe replacement below. Provider marks must additionally be current, officially supplied artwork used only for the provider function they identify.

This is an engineering policy based on published terms, not legal advice. Product counsel/brand approval remains necessary where an organization relies on a private commercial agreement.

## Asset gate for implementation

Every shipped visual asset must have one manifest row containing: stable asset id; class and UI purpose; copyright/trademark owner; authoritative source URL or internal DAM id; license/contract identifier and retrieval date; allowed use and modification constraints; required attribution/notices; original and derived SHA-256; transformation recipe; reviewer; and replacement/expiry trigger. CI should reject unmanifested font, SVG, raster, and animation files and reject the four legacy SF/Bebas hashes above.

Provider assets require a periodic/current-version check and must be removed when the integration is disabled. Third-party license texts ship in the repository/distribution as required. Visual parity tests assert layout, recognizable purpose, dimensions, color role, and motion timing; they must not force reuse of an unproven binary or path data.

## Resolution

Published evidence supports a clean upstream OFL replacement for Bebas Neue, but not redistribution of the legacy Bebas subset itself. Apple's terms rule out shipping the bundled, modified SF Pro webfonts. Rights remain unestablished for the password-mask font, all 96 legacy SVG files, and the absent Lottie content. Full parity is consequently conditional on licensed/provider retrieval or independently owned replacements, with native password masking and generic payment UI as safe fallbacks.
