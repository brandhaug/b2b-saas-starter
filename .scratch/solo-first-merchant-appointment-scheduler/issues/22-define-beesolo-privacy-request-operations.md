# Define beesolo Privacy Request Operations

Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Blocked by: 09

## Question

What public intake, exact-destination verification, System Operator permission and review, ambiguity handling, retention-hold, correction, export delivery, erasure execution, Merchant notice, audit, abuse-prevention, and recovery contract should operate beesolo-managed Privacy Requests without requiring Customer Accounts or Merchant approval?

## Comments

### Resolution — 2026-08-02

beesolo operates an accountless public **Privacy Request** workflow for Access, Customer Data Correction, and Customer Data Erasure. The Merchant remains controller for Merchant-scoped Customer Records and Appointment customer data; beesolo acts as processor under standing documented instructions in the Merchant's accepted Data Processing Agreement, so no case requires Merchant approval. beesolo is independently controller only for its own security, operator-access, abuse-prevention, and legally required audit data. Production release requires counsel approval of the Data Processing Agreement, controller contact details, public privacy notice, retention schedule, and rejection language; [Process Privacy Requests under standing Merchant instructions](../../../docs/adr/0073-process-privacy-requests-under-standing-instructions.md) records the architectural boundary.

The bilingual Privacy Request Intake is linked from the Public Site, every Public Booking Page, confirmation view, and privacy notice. After Turnstile it collects only request type, Merchant booking-page URL or exact public slug, one Customer Record email or phone, and preferred language. Receipt immediately creates a **Submitted** case and starts the governing deadline before verification; it never waits to discover whether a matching record exists. Before verification beesolo collects no explanation, replacement value, Appointment facts, or identity document. The requester has seventy-two hours to verify before the case becomes **Expired**, and every pre-verification response remains identical whether the Merchant, destination, or Customer Record exists.

Exact-destination proof uses a ten-minute one-time code with five attempts. Email uses essential security email and phone uses plain SMS through a separate platform-funded **Verification Delivery** capability, not WhatsApp, Appointment Notifications, Merchant Messaging Balance, or consent. Production SMS remains subject to live-route qualification; provider-light development is safely unavailable or test-only. Enhanced verification adds a linked Confirmation Access Token, proof of another historical destination, or evidence spanning multiple relevant Appointments. Shared destinations, conflicting identifiers, merge or split history, holds, bans, and inconclusive proof are always enhanced-review risks. Launch collects no government ID, selfie, or payment data, and insufficient evidence is rejected without confirming that a record exists.

Public abuse controls use Turnstile, generic responses, rotatable keyed Merchant-and-destination fingerprints, and configurable network limits. One destination may receive three codes per hour and six per day, with at most three open cases for the same Merchant; compatible verified duplicates coalesce. Repeated abuse raises a risk flag but an authorized operator may admit a legitimate request through separately verified evidence. A manifestly unfounded or excessive request may be rejected only by a reviewer with a recorded reason and non-disclosing requester notice; launch charges no fee.

The audited lifecycle is **Submitted → Awaiting Verification → Queued for Review → Awaiting Additional Evidence | Approved → Executing → Completed**, with **Rejected**, **Withdrawn**, and **Expired** terminal alternatives. beesolo targets fourteen-calendar-day completion and tracks the governing one-month deadline from initial receipt. A justified extension, evidence request, or rejection requires an authorized operator, a reason, and requester notice and never silently pauses the clock. Required localized notices cover receipt, verification outcome, evidence need, extension and new deadline, material hold or execution delay, completion, rejection with complaint and judicial-remedy information, withdrawal, and expiry. Required-notice delivery failure creates operator attention and is not successful communication.

Operations uses three explicit permissions. `privacy-request:read` sees the masked deadline-and-risk-ordered queue. `privacy-request:review` claims and reveals the necessary case data, requests evidence, records extensions or rejection, releases an Access package, approves correction, and performs a privacy-scoped identity partition. `privacy-request:erase` additionally approves irreversible erasure. Every sensitive action authoritatively rechecks current permission and a recently two-factor-authenticated Operator Session. An operator must hold an exclusive thirty-minute renewable claim before viewing personal data or deciding a case; inactivity releases it, reasoned takeover is audited, and revision plus idempotency guards prevent stale or duplicate actions. Launch does not require a second operator; the approved system workflow performs execution.

Every approval binds to an immutable **Privacy Request Preflight** containing verified identity scope, risk flags, Customer Record graph and merge provenance, holds, bans, future Appointments and queue activity, queued Notifications, artifacts, provider obligations, policy version, and source-data revision. Access previews the exact package and exclusions, Correction previews the exact mutation plan, and Erasure fixes the target manifest and retained-fact explanation. A relevant identity, hold, destination, merge, Appointment, or policy change invalidates approval and returns the case to review rather than silently changing the result.

When a mistaken merge combines people and proof identifies only part of the record, `privacy-request:review` may execute a narrow **Privacy Identity Partition** without Merchant approval. It assigns only proven Appointments, observations, contacts, consent, and notes to a new record, preserves immutable snapshots and merge provenance, and quarantines uncertain data from disclosure. Erasing the partition additionally requires `privacy-request:erase`. This is the sole privacy exception to the Owner-controlled Customer Record Split and grants no authority for ordinary deduplication or preference decisions.

An approved **Customer Data Package** is a point-in-time artifact with a human-readable HTML index and machine-readable JSON and CSV files. It applies the exclusions already fixed by the Customer Directory contract, including Merchant Notes, private ban reasons, security signals, other customers' data, internal audit metadata, and secrets. The artifact is encrypted at rest, has no public object URL, and is never attached or exposed through an email bearer link. A non-sensitive availability notice goes to the verified destination; fresh exact-destination verification creates a fifteen-minute case-scoped resumable download session. The artifact expires after seven days and may be regenerated from then-current retained data before the request deadline. Generation, notification, verification, completed download, expiry, and regeneration are separate evidence. Access becomes **Completed** when the approved package exists and its availability notice is delivered; download is optional.

An approved **Customer Data Correction** states the incorrect and replacement values. A replacement email or phone must itself pass exact-destination verification before becoming preferred or contactable. The Customer Record changes prospectively and the old contact becomes **Disputed** or **Superseded**, excluding it from matching, search, and communication. Historical Appointment snapshots do not change by default; the requester may explicitly select future Scheduled Appointments for the existing audited destination-change workflow. Removing data from completed history is Erasure. Correction never silently transfers consent, mutates queued Notifications or Confirmation access, changes bans or Merchant Notes, or touches another record. It completes only after every approved mutation commits, destination and Notification consequences reconcile, and its requester notice is delivered.

Approved **Customer Data Erasure** immediately quarantines the Customer Record from matching and suppresses unsent communication, then executes a fixed manifest of authoritative records, snapshot fields, generated artifacts, caches, notification destinations, and provider deletion obligations. Each target deletes or anonymizes independently and idempotently with durable evidence. Partial failure stays **Executing** and retries without rolling back already-erased data. Completion requires every manifest target and provider obligation, all holds, a post-erasure scan showing no prohibited value remains searchable or deliverable, and successful requester notice. A minimal non-identifying tombstone may preserve idempotency, audit linkage, and permitted suppression or active-ban fingerprints but never erased values.

A **Retention Hold** is narrowly scoped to named records or data classes and records its legal or dispute reason, authority or case reference, responsible operator, creation time, and review or expiry date. Privacy reviewers may inspect but cannot create, clear, or override it; those actions require the separate `governance:retention-hold` permission. Access and valid prospective correction continue unless disclosure itself is restricted. Unheld data erases immediately, held data stays isolated, and the case remains **Executing** until the hold clears and residual erasure completes. Renewal, narrowing, and release are independently audited.

Withdrawal before approval stops the case; Access withdrawal revokes and deletes an undownloaded package; Erasure withdrawal before its first mutation releases quarantine. Once a correction or erasure mutation commits, beesolo never recreates deleted or superseded data. It stops remaining uncommitted work where safe, prevents held data from later erasure under that request, and records **Withdrawn — partial irreversible execution** with a precise outcome notice. A later request creates a new case rather than reopening the old one.

Access creates no Merchant notice. Correction sends one post-commit notice only when Merchant-visible current details or future Appointments changed. Erasure sends an immediate post-quarantine notice when upcoming Appointments or active queue entries are affected and a completion notice. These notices use opaque references and safe Merchant App links and contain no old or replacement value, request proof, private reason, operator identity, hold detail, or downloadable data. The Merchant may adapt operations but cannot approve, reject, delay, reverse, or contact the requester through the workflow. Merchant-notice failure remains an operational alert without rolling back or blocking the privacy action.

The immutable **Privacy Request Audit Trail** records intake, verification outcomes, risk flags, claims and takeovers, sensitive-data reveals, evidence exchanges, decisions, lifecycle changes, correction and erasure steps, holds, package delivery, Merchant notices, retries, and recovery. It retains stable non-identifying actor, case, and affected-record references, state and event facts, permission and policy versions, reason codes, and outcomes while excluding raw customer values, codes, package contents, provider payloads, and free-text evidence. Verification secrets disappear on use or expiry; request free text and direct delivery destinations purge thirty days after terminal resolution; value-free case, decision, erasure, and audit evidence remains three years, then deletes or becomes non-identifying aggregate metrics.

Recovery uses durable automatic retry and operator-visible incidents; manual retry or skip requires the original action's permission, fresh two-factor authentication, and a reason, and no required erasure target may be skipped into success. A value-free **Privacy Action Ledger** lives outside the primary Merchant-data restore boundary. D1 restore stays in maintenance mode until every correction, quarantine, erasure, and active hold absent from the restore point is replayed, expired packages are invalidated, provider obligations resume, and the post-erasure scan passes. Restoring backup history never makes an erased value searchable, deliverable, or writable again.
