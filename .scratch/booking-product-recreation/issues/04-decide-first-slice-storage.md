# Decide First-Slice Storage

Type: grilling
Status: done
Blocked by: 02

## Question

Which first-slice entities need D1 persistence, which can remain derived or seeded, and how should seed data be structured so local development, tests, and screenshots exercise the same Booking Vertical Slice?

Resolve the persistence boundary before detailed schema work: merchant-managed data, public booking state, customer identity/contact details, carts, appointments, sale orders, availability, and confirmation records.

## Decision

- D1 is the source of truth for all mutable Booking Vertical Slice state, including the seeded demo merchant and unfinished Booking Sessions.
- Seed data is deterministic initial data, not an alternative runtime store. Local development, end-to-end tests, and screenshots populate D1 from the same canonical booking fixture; unit tests may load that fixture into in-memory capability adapters.
- Runtime applications do not silently fall back to fixture data.
- Derived concepts such as Booking Readiness and Availability are computed from persisted inputs rather than stored independently.
- Brand and Shop remain conceptual future structure and have no first-slice tables, foreign keys, or seed rows. The first slice persists the Merchant and its directly owned booking configuration; Legacy Source shop data is translated into the Merchant's public identity rather than preserved as a placeholder Shop.
- Merchant and Public Booking Page are separate persisted records with a required one-to-one relationship. Merchant owns the canonical `merchantSlug` and business identity; Public Booking Page owns customer-facing content and Published or Unpublished status. Booking Readiness is derived, and the first slice has no draft, revision, or published-snapshot storage.
- Merchant, not Workspace, is the Booking Product tenant and authorization boundary. Workspace and the Workspace-to-Merchant bridge are absent from the target storage model. Better Auth identity and session records remain, while useful membership mechanics may be adapted to Merchant ownership without retaining Starter domain entities.
- Merchant authorization uses a persisted Merchant Member relation rather than an `ownerUserId` column. First-slice constraints make the relation one-to-one: each Merchant has exactly one Merchant Owner and each authenticated person owns at most one Merchant. Merchant Members are operators with product access and remain distinct from bookable Providers; additional members and multi-Merchant ownership require a later migration and explicit Merchant selection.
- Every bookable person is a persisted Provider, including a Solo Merchant's default Provider. Solo may hide provider management in the UI, but Services, eligibility, Schedule Rules, Booking Sessions, and Appointments use the same Provider model as Team merchants. A Merchant Member and Provider may correspond to the same person without becoming the same record.
- Provider-Service eligibility is explicit persisted Merchant Catalog configuration, including eligibility for the Solo default Provider. It is not inferred as "every Provider performs every Service" and is not stored as Provider ID arrays inside Service records.
- Scheduling persists the Merchant's IANA timezone and recurring weekly Schedule Rules per Provider. Availability and Time Slots are derived on request from those rules, Service duration, Provider-Service eligibility, confirmed Appointment conflicts, and unexpired Time Slot Holds; generated slots are never persisted or seeded. Tests and screenshot capture use an injected fixed clock so the same rules produce deterministic future slots.
- A Booking Session is a complete server-owned working aggregate persisted in D1: selected Services, Provider Preference, selected Time Slot, Customer Details, Checkout Path, expiry, lifecycle status, and a hash of its browser-held capability secret. The server assigns the fixed first-slice Checkout Path at session creation and returns it read-only; browser confirmation commands cannot select or replace it. The cookie contains only the secret used with the opaque session ID; URLs and browser form state are not sources of truth. The first slice stores no Booking Session event log or revision history.
- Successful confirmation retains the Booking Session for 24 hours in a terminal Confirmed state linked to its Appointment rather than deleting it immediately. Its capability hash may authorize only an identical confirmation replay, which returns the existing result instead of creating another Appointment; every other read or mutation is rejected. The terminal record is then purged, and no separate Sale Order record is introduced.
- An Appointment retains references to its current Merchant Catalog records and immutable snapshots of the facts accepted at confirmation: Service names, durations, prices and currency; Provider display name; scheduled start and end with Merchant timezone; the original Provider Preference path; Customer Details; and Checkout Path. Later catalog edits cannot rewrite Appointment history or change an existing Confirmation.
- The first slice has no durable Customer record or Customer table. Customer Details are persisted on the Booking Session and snapshotted on the Appointment. The Merchant App Customers screen is an appointment-derived Customer Directory and must not infer that matching email or phone values are one durable identity; customer accounts, deduplication, merging, and CRM behavior remain deferred.
- Confirmation has no separate persisted record. It is a read model derived from the immutable Appointment snapshot. Confirmation-specific persistence is limited to access metadata linked to the Appointment: opaque route ID, token version, expiry, signing-key ID, and revocation state. The HMAC-derived Confirmation Access Token is never persisted; the route ID locates the view but does not authorize access.
- Selecting a Time Slot creates an expiring Time Slot Hold within the active Booking Session rather than recording a non-exclusive preference or creating a separate reservation record. Availability excludes unexpired holds, and confirmation atomically converts the winning hold into an Appointment; expiry releases it automatically.
- For an Any Provider booking, creating the Time Slot Hold atomically assigns a concrete eligible Provider. The Booking Session preserves Any Provider as the customer's original Provider Preference while storing the concrete assignment used for conflict detection and the eventual Appointment; assignment is not deferred until checkout or confirmation.
- An unexpired Time Slot Hold also carries a Booking Quote: a snapshot of the selected Service facts, durations, prices and currency, plus the assigned Provider facts accepted when the hold was created. Merchant edits or unpublishing do not change that quote. Hold expiry also expires the quote; a new hold uses current catalog data. Confirmation copies the accepted Booking Quote into the Appointment snapshot.
- Services and Providers persist an Active or Inactive lifecycle instead of being hard-deleted after use. Inactive records are excluded from new Booking Sessions and Booking Readiness, while existing Booking Quotes, Appointments, and Confirmations remain valid through snapshots. The first slice does not introduce generic soft-delete metadata.
- First-slice Schedule Rules contain recurring weekly Provider hours only. One-off exceptions such as holidays, time off, and extra hours—and their precedence and editing behavior—are deferred to a later Scheduling slice rather than represented by unused first-slice tables.
- Terminal Booking Sessions do not retain their complete private payload indefinitely. Expired abandoned sessions are deleted after a short cleanup window. Confirmed sessions are reduced for 24 hours to idempotency records containing only session ID, terminal status, Appointment ID, capability hash, and timestamps, after which the terminal hash is removed; Customer Details and the accepted Booking Quote remain only on the Appointment snapshot.
- This storage decision introduces neither Sale Order nor a generic Payment ledger. Booking Session and Appointment persistence includes the fixed `pay_in_person` Checkout Path and accepted money snapshots; issue 09 defers all provider-specific payment state.
- The first slice persists no Merchant Checkout Policy. Pay In Person is the fixed product Checkout Path, so Booking Readiness has no checkout-configuration input and environment configuration cannot alter booking behavior.
- The canonical Seed Booking Scenario is one connected, Published and fully bookable Merchant graph: one Merchant Owner; two Providers including the default; three Services with differing Provider eligibility; recurring weekly Schedule Rules; one past Appointment for history and the Customer Directory; one future Appointment that blocks Availability; and one set of Confirmation access metadata for confirmation-view tests. It contains no Checkout Policy, active Booking Sessions, Time Slot Holds, or pending outbox work. Tests derive Unpublished, incomplete, and Solo variants from this base rather than adding unrelated demo Merchants to the canonical D1 seed.
- One typed Seed Booking Scenario builder is the only authored fixture source. The local seed script translates that graph into Drizzle-backed D1 rows, capability tests load it into in-memory adapters, and end-to-end tests and screenshots use seeded local D1. Test and story variants transform the base graph; handwritten SQL and app-specific copies of the booking fixture are not maintained.
- The Seed Booking Scenario builder requires an explicit `anchorTime` and never reads the system clock internally. Local seeding may deliberately pass the current week to keep appointments relevant, while CI, tests, and screenshots pass a fixed anchor. The same anchor always produces identical IDs, dates, Availability, and Confirmation data.
- Rerunning `db:seed` transactionally replaces only the stable Seed Booking Scenario Merchant graph through its ownership boundary, then recreates it from the typed fixture. It preserves unrelated local Merchants and cannot leave stale seeded Services or Appointments behind after fixture changes.
- A Time Slot Hold and its Booking Quote expire at a fixed ten-minute `expiresAt` measured from hold creation. Reads do not extend the deadline. After expiry the Booking Session may continue, but it must acquire a new slot and current quote; issue 09 may define a separate protected state for an in-flight external payment.
- An unfinished anonymous Booking Session expires after 30 minutes without activity and has a two-hour absolute lifetime. Expiry invalidates its capability and requires a new session; its ten-minute Time Slot Hold may expire earlier and is never extended by session activity.
- A Confirmation Access Token expires 30 days after its Appointment ends and may be revoked earlier. It remains valid if the Merchant unpublishes its Public Booking Page, but it is never a permanent bearer link to Appointment and Customer Details.
- Confirmation is one atomic D1 capability operation: consume the valid Time Slot Hold, create the Appointment and its immutable snapshots, issue the Confirmation Access Token, reduce the Booking Session to its Confirmed idempotency record, and append the durable outbox item. The outbox record is persisted; the queue remains only a wake-up signal as settled in the topology decision.
